Amader Privacy Policy

Effective date: [DATE]

Amader is run by Eiliyah Sarowar ("Amader", "we", "us"). Amader helps families share one meal a day and keep an eye on one food together. It is not a medical app. It does not ask for health readings, diagnoses or medicines, and it does not give medical advice.

This policy covers the Amader app on iPhone and Android and the web version of the app. If you live in Washington State or Nevada, please also read our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, which says the same things in the form those states require.

Questions or requests: eiliyahsa@gmail.com. Mail: Eiliyah Sarowar, PMB 351466, 2301 Vanderbilt Place, Nashville, TN 37235-1466.

The short version

  • Your meals and photos are shared with your family table and nobody else.
  • We never sell your information, never use it for ads, and do not use advertising or analytics trackers.
  • An AI service reads a meal photo only after you say yes, and you can turn it off.
  • "Help Amader learn our food" is off unless you turn it on.
  • When you measure with your hand, the hand is worked out on your phone and never saved or sent to us.
  • During the pilot we count simple things per family, such as which days the app was opened and how often a photo guess was fixed, to learn what works. This stays with Amader.
  • Your family's meals stay while your family uses Amader. A family with no meals for 12 months is deleted. You can delete any meal, or everything, at any time in the app.

What we collect

What you type or choose - Your first name or what your family calls you, and whether you are a parent or a son or daughter. - When you set up a family: the name you use for your parent, the one food your family keeps an eye on (rice by default), and the usual amount. - Your language. This is kept on your phone only.

What you share - For each meal: which starch it had (for example rice, roti, bread or none), how much in your own handfuls, pieces or slices, when you ate it (date, time and breakfast, lunch, dinner or snack), and, once that step is on your path, whether you started with vegetables. - A photo of the meal, if you add one. - Hearts and short notes you send to your family. - Your path: the small step you picked (for example "at most 2½ handfuls at a meal"), which step you are on and since when. Your family's shared grain count and the costumes it bought for Mado. - In the full version of the app (not the pilot), also: voice notes, family recipes, who you ate with, other food groups on the plate, quest ticks and dishes you teach the app.

What the app works out - If you turn on photo reading: a short dish name (for example "Dal, rice and sabzi"), a rough split of the plate into grains, vegetables and dal, protein, and other, and a guess of the goal food amount. - Weekly recaps and the checkup summary are built from your own meals.

Technical information - A random sign-in id for your phone or browser. Amader has no accounts, passwords, emails or phone numbers. - Your family code, which lets your phone see your family's table. - Our service providers' servers record basic connection details such as IP address, device type and time of request, as nearly every website and app does. - Two small counters used only to stop abuse: wrong family codes typed (deleted after 1 day) and photos sent for reading (deleted after 2 days). - A few usage records, each with your seat at the family and a time: the app was opened (once a day), a photo guess was made, a photo guess was changed, and the "What is a starch?" help was opened. They are only read by Amader, never shown to your family, and are deleted with the family table. - If you turn on notifications: your phone's push address, so your family's "Ammi shared dinner" can reach you. - A "New phone?" link, if someone in your family makes one for you: a random code that works once and expires after one day.

What we do not collect - Location. Photos are re-saved on your phone before upload, which removes GPS and other hidden details. - Contacts, health readings, medicines, diagnoses or payment details. - Your hand shape (see "Measuring with your hand" below).

How we use it

  • To show your meals to your family table, and your family's meals to you.
  • To build your weekly recap and your checkup summary.
  • To read meal photos, only if you said yes.
  • To improve how Amader recognises home cooking, only if you turned on "Help Amader learn our food".
  • To keep the service secure, stop abuse and fix problems.
  • To meet legal duties.

We do not use your information for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not share it with anyone for their own marketing.

Who can see what

Your family table. Everyone who joined your family with the family code can see the names, meals, photos, portions, dish names and hearts shared at that table. Treat the family code like a house key.

Your checkup summary. It is made on your phone and only leaves your phone if you choose to share it, for example with a doctor. It lists your own meals and may include a few of your own plate photos.

Your path. The step you picked and your progress are shown to your family, like your meals.

People who run Amader. Eiliyah Sarowar can open the database and photo storage to run and fix the service, keep it secure, answer your requests and, during the pilot, see the usage counts above. We do not browse your photos. We open one only to fix a problem you reported, to deal with abuse, or with your permission.

People outside your family. Nobody else can open your family's photos. Photos are kept in private storage and open only through links that expire after an hour.

Measuring with your hand

"Measure with my hand" looks at your hand in a meal photo to use your fist as a ruler. This happens entirely on your phone or in your browser on your phone. The positions of your hand are used for that one measurement and then thrown away. They are never saved, never sent to us, never sent to any other company, and never used to identify you. We do not collect, store or share any scan of hand or face geometry. If this ever changes, we will ask for your written permission first and publish how long we keep it.

The meal photo itself may show your hand, the same as any photo. It is handled like any other meal photo in this policy.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Amader tells your family when you share a meal, and tells you when someone sends you a note. These go from our server through Expo's push service and then Apple or Google to the phone. They carry only a first name and the meal time, for example "Ammi shared dinner. Send a heart?". They never contain what you ate, a note's words or a photo. Reminders like "Did you eat?" are set on your phone and do not go through us.

Read aloud

The listen buttons use your phone's or browser's own voice. They only read words that are part of the app, such as food names, never your meals or names. Some browsers use their maker's online voice service for this.

Photo reading by AI

The first time you add a photo, Amader asks whether it may read the photo to guess the starch on the plate and how much, and to name the dish. Until you say yes, no photo is sent. If you say yes, the photo is sent from our server to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI, which returns the guess. No person at Amader looks at the photo for this. You can turn it off any time under Me, "Guess what's on my plate".

Anthropic processes these photos for us under its commercial terms. Under those terms Anthropic does not use them to train its AI models, and deletes them from its systems within 30 days, except that it may keep information longer (up to 2 years) if it is flagged for a possible violation of Anthropic's usage policies, or where the law requires. "Help Amader learn our food"

This is off unless you turn it on, under Me. If you turn it on, each new meal photo you share also adds a learning example: what the app guessed, the amount you saved, and whether you kept the guess, tapped a number or measured with your hand. The example points at your own photo. Your family's photos are never included.

We use these examples only to improve how Amader recognises home cooking. We do not sell them or give them to anyone else. In the future we may use them to train our own recognition model; that model would not contain your photos or your name.

Turn it off and your examples are deleted. Deleting a meal deletes its example too, and so does "Delete my meals and leave".

If your family table is deleted because nobody shared a meal for 12 months, we keep the learning examples you shared, so Amader can keep learning. We keep them without your name, your family, your family code or your sign-in id, and with only the month they were shared. After that they can no longer be linked to you, so we cannot find them to delete them on request. We keep these for as long as they are useful for improving food recognition. A photo can still show what is in it, such as a hand, a table or a room, so only share photos you are comfortable with.

Your choices and rights

  • Delete a meal: tap "Remove" under your meal on Our Table. The photo is deleted too.
  • Delete everything: Me, "Delete my meals and leave". This removes your meals, photos, hearts, learning examples and your seat at the table.
  • Turn off photo reading or learning examples: Me, Photos.
  • Get a copy or ask a question: email eiliyahsa@gmail.com. Because Amader has no accounts, we will ask for your family code and name to find your information, and may ask you to confirm from the app.

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or get a copy of your information, to withdraw consent, and to appeal if we say no. We honour these requests for everyone, wherever they live. If we turn down a request you may appeal by replying to our answer; if you are still unhappy you may contact your state attorney general.

We will not treat you differently for using these rights.

The pilot

During the first pilot, families who take part get $30 as a thank-you. Amader uses the usage counts above, and anything you choose to tell us in conversation, to learn what works. We do not publish anything that names you or your family. If we want to quote you, we ask first.

Age

Amader is for adults 18 and older. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If you think a child has used Amader, email us and we will delete it.

Security

We use private photo storage, short-lived photo links, database rules that only let a phone see the families it joined, encrypted connections, and limits that stop people guessing family codes. No system is perfect. If a breach affects your information we will tell you as the law requires, and as quickly as we can.

Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top and show a notice in the app. If a change means we would use your information in a new way, for example sharing learning examples with a partner, we will ask for your permission first.
